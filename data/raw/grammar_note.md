# Nadi-9 Dialect Grammar Note
**Prepared by:** Language Research Initiative
**Dialect Code:** NDI-09 (Fictional Coastal Mountain Dialect)
**Scope:** 6 Core Sections on Syntax, Tense, Negation, Politeness, Code-Switching, and Numbers.

---

### Section 1: Basic Word Order (Syntax)
- Standard Nadi-9 follows strict **Subject-Object-Verb (SOV)** word order in formal and declarative statements.
  - Example: *Maya (Subject) pani-la (Object + marker) chora-te (Verb + present).* -> "Maya drinks water."
- The marker `-la` attaches to the direct object noun.
- In rapid conversational speech or code-switching with trade dialect, occasional **SVO** is heard, but formal register maintains **SOV**.

---

### Section 2: Tense and Aspect Markers
Tense attaches as a suffix to the verb root:
- **Present / Habitual:** `-te` (or bare root in casual dialogue).
- **Past (Completed):** `-ka` (e.g., *fala-ka* = "went/came").
- **Future / Intended:** `-ren` (e.g., *chora-ren* = "will drink").
- **Perfective / Return:** `-zho` indicates return or completed arrival.

---

### Section 3: Negation System
- Standard negation is marked by the prefix **`ni-`** directly attached to the verb stem.
  - Example: *ni-chora-ka* -> "did not drink".
- Colloquial negation particle **`nira`** precedes the verb in informal speech.
- Double negation is strictly ungrammatical in Nadi-9.

---

### Section 4: Politeness, Kinship, and Social Hierarchy
Honorifics and kinship titles are sensitive to status and family dynamics:
- **Elder Brother:**
  - Standard familial: `toran`.
  - Reconciled / High respect: `toran-ya` or `toran-ma` (suffix `-ma` conveys deep deference).
  - *Caution:* Archaic vendor notes cite `koba`, but native speakers dispute `koba` as a slang or pejorative label for an estranged sibling.
- **Elder Sister:** `sarina` / `sarina-ma`.
- **Parent / Elder:** Suffix `-ma` is mandatory when addressing anyone of superior generational standing.

---

### Section 5: Code-Switching and Contact Language
- When speakers switch to regional trade dialect or English loanwords, noun loans often take the prefix `q-` or remain unchanged.
- Humorous or ironical expressions often borrow source terms directly to signify emotional distance.

---

### Section 6: Numbers and Quantifiers
- Cardinal numbers: `uno` (one), `dura` (two), `tria` (three), `penta` (five).
- Quantifier precedes the noun: *dura toran* = "two elder brothers".
- Indefinite count: `vola` = "some/few", `mori` = "many".
